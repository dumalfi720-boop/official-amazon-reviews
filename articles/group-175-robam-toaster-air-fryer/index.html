What to Look for When Buying a Home Appliances in 2026

The evolution of the modern kitchen has led to the rise of multi-functional appliances designed to consolidate several tools into a single footprint. For the home chef, the challenge lies in finding a device that balances capacity with precision. The shift toward combi-steam technology represents a significant leap, moving beyond simple toaster ovens to offer controlled moisture and high-velocity airflow.

In this technical analysis, we examine the capabilities of the Robam ecosystem, focusing on the CT766 and its related variants. By evaluating heat distribution, interface intuitiveness, and the synergy between steam and convection, we aim to determine which configuration best serves specific culinary needs. Our approach prioritizes empirical performance metrics over marketing claims to provide an impartial guide for the discerning consumer.

When selecting a combi oven, the primary consideration should be the volume-to-power ratio. A larger capacity, such as 1.2 cubic feet, is essential for roasting whole proteins, but it requires precise temperature control to avoid uneven hotspots. Verify that the appliance offers independent controls for steam and convection to ensure versatility across different food textures.

Additionally, evaluate the user interface and the number of integrated presets. A smart touch control system reduces the learning curve, while multi-stage cooking capabilities allow for complex recipes—such as steaming followed by searing—without manual intervention. Ensure the internal materials are high-grade stainless steel for longevity and easier sanitization after high-temperature cycles.

Tips to Maximize Your Product's Lifespan

To maximize the lifespan of your combi oven, always perform a descaling cycle if you use the steam function frequently to prevent mineral buildup. When air frying, place the basket in the center rack to ensure optimal airflow. For the best results with multi-stage cooking, preheat the unit for five minutes to ensure the internal temperature is stabilized before the first cycle begins.
