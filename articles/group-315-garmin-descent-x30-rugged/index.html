🔍 People Also Ask

❓ What is the difference between a dive watch and a dive computer?
A dive watch is a pressure-resistant timepiece with basic depth indication, designed for water resistance during swimming or casual snorkeling. A dive computer actively monitors depth, decompression status, and nitrogen saturation in real-time, calculating safe ascent rates and warning you before dangerous decompression situations occur—essential for scuba diving safety.
❓ How deep can Garmin dive computers go?
The Garmin Descent™ X30 supports depths to 100 meters (328 feet), while the Descent Mk2S reaches 100 meters as well. Both models exceed recreational diving limits; professional technical versions extend beyond 200 meters with appropriate training and equipment.
❓ Do I need a dive computer if I use dive tables?
While experienced divers can use printed tables, dive computers offer significant advantages: real-time pressure consumption tracking, automatic multi-dive calculations eliminating manual math, and continuous decompression monitoring that adjusts for actual conditions. Computers reduce calculation errors and provide critical safety redundancy.
❓ Can I use my smartwatch as a dive computer?
Most smartwatches lack the specialized sensors, decompression algorithms, and safety certifications required for reliable diving. Garmin's dive computers integrate smartwatch features while maintaining professional diving accuracy; using non-certified devices underwater creates dangerous misjudgments about depth, time, and decompression safety.
❓ How often should I service my dive computer?
Professional servicing every 2-3 years maintains sensor accuracy and waterproof seal integrity. However, consistent fresh-water rinsing after saltwater dives and proper storage typically extend functional lifespan to 5+ years without service. Always follow manufacturer recommendations specific to your device model.
