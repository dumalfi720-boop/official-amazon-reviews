Zetsumei Ougi Fatalities: Execution and Strategy

The samurai shodown special introduces Zetsumei Ougi — one-hit kill finishing moves requiring specific conditions. Each character has a unique command input, typically a complex motion plus heavy slash at close range with full Rage Gauge. Genjuro's "Sanren Satsu" requires quarter-circle-back twice plus heavy slash. Haohmaru's "Tenha Fuujin Zan" demands half-circle-back twice plus heavy slash. These moves deal 100% damage but leave the user vulnerable during startup. Tournament play rarely sees them land against experienced opponents. However, they create psychological pressure — opponents must respect the threat, altering spacing decisions. Practice mode includes command display for learning inputs. Mastering one Zetsumei Ougi per main character adds high-stakes comeback potential to your arsenal.

Rage Gauge Mechanics and Powder System Deep Dive

The Rage Gauge fills as you take damage, maxing at 100% after absorbing roughly 40% health. At max, your character flashes red, gains 25% attack boost, and accesses Weapon Flipping Technique (WFT) — a super move consuming the gauge. Samurai Shodown Special adds the Powder System: pressing light slash + light kick simultaneously spends 30% Rage to temporarily increase movement speed and reduce recovery frames for 8 seconds. This enables pressure strings impossible at neutral speed. Powder activation also cancels recovery on whiffed heavy slashes, creating bait opportunities. Managing Rage between WFT threat and Powder aggression defines high-level play. The gauge carries between rounds, so taking early damage can empower late-round comebacks. Understanding exact frame advantages during Powder state separates intermediate from advanced players.

Weapon Clash and Disarm Mechanics Explained

Weapon clashes occur when two weapon-based attacks connect simultaneously — a signature Samurai Shodown mechanic. The game enters a rapid button-mash struggle; winner retains weapon, loser suffers 12-frame stun and loses weapon for 180 frames. Disarmed characters cannot use heavy slash, special moves requiring weapon, or WFT. They gain a barehanded moveset with faster light attacks but 30% damage reduction. Weapon automatically returns after 180 frames or upon scoring a knockdown. Certain characters like Earthquake and Gen-an suffer less disarmed due to strong barehanded kits. Intentionally triggering clashes when ahead on life forces opponent into unfavorable resource management. The clash system adds a unique risk-reward layer absent in Street Fighter or King of Fighters, making spacing around heavy slash startup frames critically important.

Display Options: CRT, Upscalers, and Modern Port Settings

Authentic Neo Geo hardware outputs 240p RGB at 59.14Hz — ideal for PVM/BVM CRTs or OSSC/RetroTINK upscalers. The ACA Neo Geo ports include "Scanline" filter (25%, 50%, 75% intensity), "CRT" curvature shader, and integer scaling options. For competitive play on modern displays, disable all filters and run at native 1080p/60 with integer scaling to avoid input lag. The Switch version in handheld mode renders at 720p with negligible latency. PC version supports 4K integer scaling (960p→3840p) for pixel-perfect clarity. Avoid bilinear filtering — it blurs the distinctive sprite work. Original hardware enthusiasts should note: Neo Geo AES requires RGB SCART or component transcoder for best quality; composite introduces severe dot crawl. The PS2 anthology (Samurai Shodown Anthology) runs at 480i with deinterlacing artifacts — least recommended for serious play.

What is the difference between Samurai Shodown Special and Samurai Shodown II? ▾
Samurai Shodown Special adds four new playable characters — Cham Cham, Genjuro Kibagami, Siegsmund, and Earthquake — plus playable bosses Mizuki and Amakusa via code. It introduces Zetsumei Ougi fatality moves, the Powder System for temporary speed boosts, rebalanced frame data for all returning fighters, and new special moves. The Rage Gauge carries between rounds in Special but resets each round in Samurai Shodown II.
Which platforms have the best version of Samurai Shodown Special for modern play? ▾
The ACA Neo Geo ports on PC (Steam), PlayStation 4, Xbox One, and Nintendo Switch offer the best modern experience with save states, rewind, scanline filters, online leaderboards, and training mode with frame data. The PC version additionally supports community mods for hitbox display and custom combo trials. Fightcade 2 on PC provides rollback netcode for competitive online play, which the official ACA ports lack.
How do you perform Zetsumei Ougi fatalities in Samurai Shodown Special? ▾
Each character has a unique Zetsumei Ougi command input requiring full Rage Gauge (100%) and close range. For example, Haohmaru executes Tenha Fuujin Zan with half-circle-back twice plus heavy slash; Genjuro uses Sanren Satsu with quarter-circle-back twice plus heavy slash. The moves have 20-30 frame startup and can be blocked or avoided, making them rare in high-level play but potent as psychological tools.
Does Samurai Shodown Special have rollback netcode on modern consoles? ▾
No, the official ACA Neo Geo ports on PlayStation 4, Xbox One, and Nintendo Switch use delay-based netcode only. The PC version similarly lacks rollback. The competitive community uses Fightcade 2 on PC, which implements rollback netcode for Samurai Shodown Special with typical 1-3 frame delay across North America and Japan connections.
What is the Powder System in Samurai Shodown Special and how does it work? ▾
The Powder System activates by pressing light slash + light kick simultaneously, consuming 30% of your Rage Gauge. For 8 seconds (approximately 480 frames), your character gains 20% faster movement speed, 3-frame reduced recovery on all normals, and heavy slash whiff recovery becomes cancellable into special moves. This enables pressure strings and combo routes impossible at normal speed, but sacrifices WFT access.

What happens when you lose your weapon in Samurai Shodown Special? ▾
When disarmed via Weapon Clash loss or specific throws, your character cannot use heavy slash, weapon-based special moves, or Weapon Flipping Technique. You gain a barehanded moveset with faster light attacks (3-4 frame startup vs 5-7 armed) but all damage is reduced by 30%. The weapon automatically returns after 180 frames (3 seconds) or immediately upon scoring a knockdown on the opponent.
